Education

Four schools, one line: from a small classroom in Jiangxi to a lecture hall in Hebei. Everything before university happened in my home province; everything after it, so far, has happened several hundred kilometres north.

Early years

Fuwa Kindergarten

Jiangxi

福娃幼儿园

The first classroom I can remember. School at that age was mostly singing, drawing, running around outside and learning to sit still for longer than five minutes — but it is where the habit of going somewhere to learn every morning started.

Primary

Beimen Primary School

Jiangxi

北门小学

Six years of reading, writing and arithmetic in my hometown. This is the stretch where reading stopped being homework and became something I did on my own — the beginning of the history books I still spend my evenings on.

High school

Zhenbai High School

Jiangxi

贞白中学

At high school I chose Physics, Chemistry and Biology as my subjects. The combination suited the way I think: physics for the rules behind things, chemistry for how materials actually behave, biology for the living systems those rules run inside. It is also the most direct preparation for an engineering degree.

Those three years were the most compressed period of study in my life so far, and the first time I had to decide what kind of work I wanted to be good at.

University

University of Emergency Management

Hebei

应急管理大学 · 地震工程与建筑安全学院

I am now studying at the School of Earthquake Engineering and Building Safety, majoring in Hydropower Engineering. Moving from Jiangxi to Hebei was the first time I lived away from home for a long stretch, and the first time I had to run my own schedule — classes, training, and everything in between.

The work is more concrete than anything I did at high school: mechanics, hydraulics, dams and water stations, and the emergency side of all of it — what happens when the water does not behave the way the drawings assumed.

02

What These Years Added Up To

Looking back at the four stages, the through-line is fairly simple: I kept choosing the harder, more technical option, and I kept being surrounded by people who took study seriously. Kindergarten to high school taught me how to learn on my own; university is teaching me how to build something that has to hold water.
