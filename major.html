Hydropower Engineering

I am studying Hydropower Engineering at the School of Earthquake Engineering and Building Safety, University of Emergency Management. This major combines civil engineering, hydraulics, water resources planning and emergency safety technology, which is one of its biggest features compared with ordinary hydropower majors in other universities.

02

Program Overview & Features

The major focuses on the whole life cycle of hydraulic structures, including dams, reservoirs, hydropower stations, flood control projects and water diversion systems. Students systematically learn the theories of hydrology, hydraulic calculation, structural mechanics, rock and soil mechanics, and seismic resistance of hydraulic buildings.

The most distinctive advantage of this program lies in its emergency management and disaster prevention orientation. Besides conventional hydraulic design, we pay special attention to the safety monitoring, risk assessment, early warning and emergency disposal of hydraulic engineering under extreme conditions such as earthquakes, heavy floods and geological failures.

We learn not only how to build water projects, but also how to evaluate hidden dangers, formulate emergency plans, and respond to sudden disasters related to water conservancy facilities. This characteristic is closely combined with the school’s core positioning as an emergency university.

03

Core Courses

Basic Engineering Courses
Higher Mathematics, College Physics, Engineering Mechanics, Material Mechanics, Structural Mechanics, Fluid Mechanics
Specialized Hydropower Courses
Engineering Hydrology, Hydraulic Calculation, Hydraulic Structure, Hydropower Station, Soil Mechanics and Foundation Engineering
Safety & Emergency Courses
Earthquake Engineering, Structural Seismic Design, Disaster Risk Assessment, Emergency Management of Hydraulic Projects
04

Training Goal & Career Direction

The training goal is to cultivate compound engineering talents who master both hydropower design knowledge and disaster emergency response capability. Graduates can engage in planning, survey, design, construction, operation, safety inspection and emergency management of water conservancy and hydropower projects.

Potential work fields include water conservancy departments, hydropower enterprises, engineering design institutes, emergency management institutions and geological disaster prevention units. The professional knowledge can also support further postgraduate study in hydraulic engineering, structural engineering, disaster prevention and mitigation engineering.
